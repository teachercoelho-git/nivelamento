Tense 1

Present Perfect

Actions that happened at an unspecified time in the past — what matters is the result NOW.

Formula
Subject + have/has + past participle
Time Diagram
Some past time
NOW
🎯
Life experience
Something done (or not) at some point in life
🔗
Result in the present
The action is over but its effect is still felt now
📅
Unfinished time period
With "today", "this week", "this year"
🆕
Recent news
Something that just happened — announcing new info
1
I have visited Japan three times.
Life experience — the exact time doesn't matter.
2
She has broken her leg, so she can't play.
Result now — she broke it in the past, but NOW she can't play.
3
They have lived here for ten years.
Unfinished time — they started in the past and STILL live here.
4
The president has announced a new policy.
Recent news — just happened.
⚡ Signal words
evernever alreadyyet justrecently sincefor so farlately

Tense 2

Present Perfect Continuous

An action that started in the past and is STILL ongoing — focusing on the duration and the process.

Formula
Subject + have/has been + verb-ing
Time Diagram
→
Start
NOW (still!)
⏳
Ongoing action
Started in the past, still happening now
💪
Visible result / tiredness
"You've been running — you're out of breath!"
📏
Duration emphasis
Used with "for" and "since" to highlight HOW LONG
1
I have been studying English for five years.
I started 5 years ago and I'm still studying now.
2
She has been crying — her eyes are red.
Visible effect — maybe she stopped, but we see the result.
3
They have been waiting since 8 o'clock.
Emphasizes the duration and the ongoing nature.
⚡ Signal words
forsince all day / all morninglately recentlyHow long...?
⚠️ VS Present Perfect:

"I have read the book." = I finished it. (result)
"I have been reading the book." = I'm still reading it. (process/duration)

Tense 3

Past Perfect

The "past before the past" — used when two things happened in the past and you need to show which one came FIRST.

Formula
Subject + had + past participle
Time Diagram — TWO past events in sequence
1st event (HAD)
2nd event (Past Simple)
NOW
1️⃣
Sequence in the past
Clarifies that event A happened before event B (both in the past)
🔔
Reported speech
Backshift: "I have done it" → She said she had done it
😔
Unreal past (wishes)
"I wish I had studied harder." / "If I had known..."
1
When I arrived at the cinema, the film had already started.
1st: film started → 2nd: I arrived
2
She was tired because she hadn't slept well.
Explains the reason — bad sleep (past) caused tiredness (past).
3
If he had called me, I would have come.
3rd conditional — unreal past situation.
4
He told me he had seen that movie before.
Reported speech backshift from Present Perfect.
⚡ Signal words
afterbefore whenby the time alreadyjust never … beforeonce

Tense 4

Past Simple

A completed action at a specific time in the past — the most common past tense for telling stories.

Formula
Subject + verb (past form)  |  Negative: didn't + base verb
Time Diagram — completed, finished, over
✓ Done (specific time)
NOW
✅
Completed action
Happened at a specific, known time in the past
📖
Narrative / sequence
Telling a story — events in chronological order
🔁
Past habits / repeated actions
"I played football every Saturday as a kid."
1
She graduated from university in 2019.
Specific time = Past Simple, not Present Perfect.
2
He woke up, made coffee, and left the house.
Sequence of completed past actions (narrative).
3
They didn't come to the party last night.
"Last night" forces Past Simple — never Present Perfect with this.
⚡ Signal words (ALWAYS force Past Simple!)
yesterdaylast night/week/year agoin 2020 when (+ specific)at that time

Tense 5

Past Continuous

An action that was IN PROGRESS at a specific moment in the past — often interrupted by another event.

Formula
Subject + was/were + verb-ing
Ongoing background action + interruption
⚡
was doing →
interruption (Past Simple)
🎬
Background setting
Describes what was happening when something else occurred
⚡
Interrupted action
Continuous = the background; Past Simple = the interrupt
🔀
Two parallel actions
"While she was cooking, he was setting the table."
1
I was sleeping when the alarm went off.
Background (sleeping) + interruption (alarm went off).
2
They were having dinner at 8 PM last night.
Action in progress at a specific past moment.
3
While he was driving, it started to rain.
Classic "while" structure — continuous + simple.
⚡ Signal words
whilewhen asat that moment at 8 PM last nightall morning

Tense 6

Future Perfect

An action that will be completed BEFORE a specific point in the future — looking forward to a finished result.

Formula
Subject + will have + past participle
Done before a future point
NOW
✓ Will have done
Future deadline
🏁
Completed before future deadline
Emphasizes that the action will be finished by a point in the future
📊
Predictions about completion
"By 2030, scientists will have discovered a cure."
1
By next June, she will have finished her degree.
The action (finishing) will be complete before "next June".
2
By the time you arrive, I will have cooked dinner.
Cooking will be done before you arrive.
3
They will have been married for 30 years next November.
Future Perfect with duration (will have been + ing also exists).
⚡ Signal words
by (next week/month/year) by the time before in (x years) by then
