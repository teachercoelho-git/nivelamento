Stage 1 • Discussion

Warm-up: What makes a teacher effective?

Discuss in English. Push for examples, not one-word answers.

  1. Think of your best teacher. What did they do that made learning easier?
  2. What is the difference between knowing English and being able to teach English?
  3. Should a teacher correct every error? Why / why not?
  4. How can you tell whether students really understood your explanation?
Advanced language challenge: Victor must naturally use at least three: scaffold, elicit, clarify, retain, internalize, misconception, cognitive load, learner autonomy.
Stage 2 • Core methodology

The teaching loop: Elicit → Clarify → Model → Practice → Feedback

1. Elicit

Find out what learners already know. Ask before telling.

2. Clarify

Make meaning, form, pronunciation or procedure unambiguous.

3. Model

Show a strong example before asking learners to perform.

4. Practice

Move from controlled practice to freer, realistic use.

5. Feedback

Confirm strengths, diagnose the gap, and create another attempt.
Trainer prompt: turn explanation into discovery

Instead of saying: “The present perfect is used for…”, give Victor examples and ask him what they have in common. Then discuss when eliciting is useful and when a direct explanation is more efficient.

Stage 3 • Trainer language

Giving instructions that learners can follow

Weak: “So, what I’d like you to do now, if you can, is perhaps get together and kind of discuss…”

Stronger: “Work in pairs. Choose one scenario. You have three minutes. Agree on one solution. Then be ready to explain why.”

CCQs vs ICQs

CCQ Concept-checking questions test meaning. Example: for “I used to work there”: “Do I work there now?”

ICQ Instruction-checking questions test the task. Example: “Are you writing or speaking?” “How many minutes do you have?”

Rewrite these instructions: “I want you guys to maybe think about this question and discuss it a little bit with the person close to you, and after that we’ll see.”

Create two CCQs for: “If I had known, I would have called you.”

Stage 4 • Error correction

Correct without taking over

Choose the lightest technique that helps the learner self-correct:

Pause / facial cue
Give the learner a chance to notice.
Recast
Learner: “He go yesterday.” Teacher: “He went yesterday?”
Elicit
“Yesterday, so… go or…?”
Delayed feedback
Collect errors during fluency work; review afterward.

Decision drill

For each case, say correct now / correct later / do not correct, then justify your decision:

  1. A learner repeatedly makes an error that blocks meaning.
  2. A learner makes one small grammar error during a confident presentation.
  3. The target of today’s exercise is past tense and a learner says “Yesterday I go…”.
  4. A nervous learner finally speaks for 30 seconds but makes several minor mistakes.
Stage 5 • Lesson design

Build from outcome backwards

Bad objective: “Teach conditionals.”
Observable objective: “By the end, learners can give three pieces of hypothetical business advice using the second conditional.”

Stage 6 • Main challenge

Microteaching: Victor becomes the teacher

Victor delivers an 8–10 minute mini-lesson in English. Choose one:

A. Language
Teach the difference between used to, be used to, and get used to.
B. Communication
Teach how to disagree professionally in a meeting.
C. Pronunciation
Teach sentence stress to make spoken English clearer.

Required: an objective, elicitation, a model/example, at least one CCQ or ICQ, learner practice, feedback, and a final check of learning.

Observer challenge

During the mini-lesson, the observer should intentionally give one weak answer and make one realistic error. Victor must diagnose it and decide whether to prompt, clarify, model, or delay correction.

Feedback formula

Evidence → Impact → Next attempt. Example: “You gave all three instructions at once. The learner forgot step two. Next time, chunk the instructions and use one ICQ.”

Teacher pathway • Homework

Victor’s first teacher portfolio

  1. Create a 30-minute lesson plan for an upper-intermediate learner with one measurable outcome.
  2. Prepare a 5-minute explanation of one difficult English point using examples before rules.
  3. Write 5 CCQs and 3 ICQs for the lesson.
  4. Record yourself teaching for 8–10 minutes. Watch it once focusing only on teacher talking time and clarity of instructions.
  5. Write a short reflection: What did the learner do because of my teaching that they could not do before?
Next development target: repeat microteaching regularly, with one focus per lesson: instructions, questioning, correction, pacing, then classroom presence.

Siglas importantes

CCQ – Concept-Checking Question → pergunta para verificar se o aluno entendeu o significado/conceito.

ICQ – Instruction-Checking Question → pergunta para verificar se o aluno entendeu o que deve fazer na atividade.

Frases úteis para você conduzir a aula

Walk me through your reasoning. → Me explique seu raciocínio.

How would you teach this? → Como você ensinaria isso?

Can you make that instruction shorter? → Consegue deixar essa instrução mais curta?

How will you check that the learner understood? → Como você vai verificar se o aluno entendeu?

Try it again, but this time... → Tente novamente, mas desta vez...

Give the learner a chance to self-correct. → Dê ao aluno a chance de se autocorrigir.
